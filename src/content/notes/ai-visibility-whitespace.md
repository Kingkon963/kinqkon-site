The idea I liked was simple. A free AI visibility checker, then a paid report for anyone who wants the fix. Type your brand, watch an assistant answer a question about it, see the gap. I ran the market research before I wrote any code, and the research killed the idea in its generic form. This is what I found, and what survived.

## The category is funded, crowded, and consolidating

Three signals in one year told me the pure feature play was over:

- Adobe acquired Semrush, closed in spring 2026.
- Sitecore acquired Scrunch AI for roughly $225M in June 2026.
- Profound raised a $96M Series C at roughly a $1B valuation.

The demand is real and large. Pew reported in June 2026 that 44% of US adults have used ChatGPT. Ahrefs reported in February 2026 that AI Overviews cut the click through rate of the top organic result by about 58%. Brands are scared, which is exactly why so many companies are already selling them reassurance.

## Three tiers, and I sit under all of them

**Tier 1, funded pure plays.** Profound (~$99 to ~$499 per month, annual only, category leader), Peec AI (Berlin, ~$89 to $495, strong competitor gap analysis), Scrunch AI (~$250 to $500, now owned by Sitecore), Athena HQ (YC W25, free tier, $295 starter), Gauge, Evertune, ZipTie (~$69 basic), Brandi AI.

**Tier 2, SEO suite add ons.** Semrush AI Visibility (~$99 per month per domain), Ahrefs Brand Radar ($199 to $699), SE Ranking (~$103 to $129), HubSpot AEO (from $50 per month with a free grader as a lead magnet). These own the keywords already and have backlink moats I cannot buy.

**Tier 3, free checkers.** Ahrefs, Semrush, HubSpot, Mangools, Birdeye, Search Atlas and SE Ranking all give it away as a funnel. Under them sit a long tail of small tools. Several rank for the exact terms I wanted, which means I would be paying to out rank HubSpot on "free AI visibility checker". I am not going to win that auction.

## What the free versions actually revealed

The part that mattered was not the price list, it was the shape of the whitespace underneath it:

1. **Non English and regional markets are underserved.** This category is built English first. Regional players barely exist. Lower competition, but also lower willingness to pay.
2. **Vertical depth beats breadth.** A single vertical with a real prompt library and a playbook specific to that industry is more useful than a generic "type your brand" box.
3. **Report only tools are the norm.** The ones that actually do something cost real money. A cheap, concrete "here is the page you should build next" output is rare at a low price.
4. **Reachability is cheap to check.** Whether AI crawlers can even read a site (robots.txt, structured data, extractability) costs nothing per check because no model call is involved.
5. **In a commodity category the moat is distribution, not features.** An embeddable badge, a public leaderboard, a genuinely unlimited free tier. The funded players cannot do unlimited free, because it damages their funnel.

## What I concluded

Direct subscription software is a hard fight against teams with real funding and against free tools backed by enormous domains. The versions that make sense for a solo build are narrower: a regional or non English entrant, a single vertical with vertical specific advice, or a comparison and free checker site that earns affiliate revenue from the incumbents instead of trying to replace them. A one off paid audit at $49 to $149 is also viable, but only if the free scan surfaces something genuinely alarming.

I built the version of this that suited me, which is the regional one.

## Caveat

I did this research in October 2026 for my own tool. Prices are vendor list prices from around August and September 2026, and this market moves fast. Re verify anything here before you quote it at somebody.
