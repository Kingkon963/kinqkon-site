I wrote this while applying for roles in Ireland, because the same conversation kept happening: a good company, a role that fits, and then a pause at the sponsorship question. The pause is almost always based on a wrong model of how the Critical Skills Employment Permit works. So here is the actual shape of it, from the official sources rather than from recruiter folklore.

## The short version

If a company can offer a two year contract at 40,904 euro or above, it is already legally able to hire a non EEA engineer. The Critical Skills Employment Permit needs no prior sponsorship history, no labour market test, and no immigration team.

## Why it is low effort for the employer

| Concern | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "We have never sponsored anyone" | Not required. Any Irish registered, Revenue registered employer can apply. First timers are welcome. |
| "We do not have an immigration team" | Not needed. The applicant prepares and drives the entire application through the government portal. |
| "What about advertising and a labour market test" | Not required for this permit. Software roles sit on the Critical Skills Occupations List. |
| "What does it cost us" | Zero. The applicant makes the application, so the processing fee and the admin sit on the applicant's side. Irish law also prohibits deducting such costs from an employee's salary. |
| "How much paperwork is it" | Roughly 30 to 60 minutes of HR time in total: an employment contract, a short employer declaration, and the company registration details. |
| "What if the role ends early" | Normal Irish employment law applies. Nothing extra is required. |

## The employer's three tasks, which is the complete list

1. **Employment contract.** Minimum two years, salary at or above 40,904 euro, role classified as an ICT professional, based in Ireland. Every software engineering role qualifies.
2. **Employer declaration form.** A short signed form with the company's registration numbers and the role details. The applicant can pre fill every field.
3. **That is it.** The applicant submits, pays, tracks, and answers any follow up queries from the department.

## The facts that actually matter

- **Fee.** 1,000 euro, paid by the applicant. 90% is refunded if the application is unsuccessful.
- **Processing.** Handled strictly in date order. Typically a number of weeks, with the current queue published. Applications should be lodged at least 12 weeks before the employment start date.
- **The 50/50 rule.** At least 50% of staff must be EEA nationals at the time of application. Start ups under two years old backed by Enterprise Ireland or IDA are exempt. Most companies with a handful of non EEA staff already satisfy this.
- **No labour market needs test** for Critical Skills roles.
- **After the permit is issued** the employer's obligations are simply to employ the person in the role named on the permit and to follow normal employment law.

## Why I wrote this down

Because the cost of the misconception is borne by the person who already has the least leverage in the conversation. A hiring manager hears "visa sponsorship" and pictures a legal project. The actual project is an employment contract and a form. Engineers reading this can send it to whoever is hesitating. Employers reading it can go and check the department's own pages, which say the same thing.

The figures here were verified against the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment pages in August 2026. Check them again before relying on them, because fees and salary thresholds change.
